# §A — Referral-program architecture (lawyer + therapist + hospital-grade)

> Law firms and therapist practices have spent 30 years refining referral networks. Gaby's playbook can borrow the structure wholesale.

### A.1 — Peer-specialist structured referral contract (the Overture / Overture.law model)
- **Source case:** Overture is a US legal platform with 6,000+ attorneys; their public guide "The Art of the Second Opinion" describes the formal infrastructure.
- **What:** Convert Gaby's 30-colleague coffee chats into written "Specialist Reciprocal Referral Agreement" (1 page). Defines:
  - Trigger conditions ("case I refer to you" vs "case you refer to me")
  - Reciprocity floor (e.g. "Gaby refers 5+ per year" to avoid one-way flow)
  - Mutual feedback loop ("I send a thank-you note within 14 days")
  - Termination clause (30-day notice)
- **Why Gaby:** 30 coffee visits without a written agreement = relationships that decay. The contract is the structure that outlasts the people.
- **Source:** `dentist/03_LAUNCH/referral-program/` already has the talk-track; this idea adds the FORMAL contract structure.
- **Effort:** 30 min to draft, 30 min per signature. 30 contracts = 15 hours over 6 weeks.

### A.2 — Niche positioning ("I am THE person for X") — therapist playbook
- **Source case:** Multiple therapist growth guides stress niche positioning.
- **What:** Gaby chooses 1–3 niches she's THE person for and leads with them publicly. From Ometz's natural assets:
  - **"Segunda opinión escrita en rehabilitación oral"** (her differentiator)
  - **"Odontología conservadora para adultos 35-65"** (her conservatism)
  - **"Cuidado dental durante embarazo"** (her gentleness + the HidroBaby partnership)
- **Convert WhatsApp templates + GBP posts + bio + website hero to lead with these.**
- **Why Gaby:** Therapists report that **niche positioning doubles referral rates within 2-3 months** because referrers remember niches. Generalist = forgettable.
- **Effort:** 2h to re-pitch messaging.

### A.3 — Hospital-style second-opinion intake form
- **Source case:** London law firms use a 1-page intake form ("What is your decision? What are the competing considerations? Who needs the second opinion?") which dramatically improves the *quality* of inbound second-opinion requests.
- **What:** Build a Google Form that Gaby's site sends every "I want a second opinion" clicker through:
  1. What decision are you trying to make? (dropdown)
  2. What did your current dentist recommend? (textarea)
  3. Have you had a panoramic X-ray taken? (file upload + checkbox)
  4. What's your age range + general health status? (radio buttons)
  5. When would you like to talk? (calendar slot)
- **Why Gaby:** Quality filter; also qualifies leads; also captures SOP for remote-second-opinion delivery.
- **Effort:** 1h setup.

### A.4 — Annual "review meeting" with top 5 referral partners
- **Source case:** B2B SaaS companies do annual QBRs (Quarterly Business Reviews) with top customers. Same logic: relationship maintenance compounds.
- **What:** Every year, invite top 5 referral colleagues to a 1-hour coffee OR sponsored dinner where you discuss:
  - "How were referrals this year? What worked / what didn't?"
  - "What's changing in your practice I should know?"
  - "Are there cases we both lost that we should be capturing?"
- **Why Gaby:** Standard B2B best practice; transfers to solo professional context.
- **Effort:** 2h once/year.

### A.5 — Reciprocal "co-counsel" / co-treatment arrangements
- **Source case:** Overture's blog: "Where second opinion grows into real, ongoing involvement, reviewing substantial work, contributing to strategy over time, it may be time to formalize it as a co-counsel or consulting arrangement, with the appropriate fee agreement."
- **What:** For 2-3 close colleagues with shared complex cases, formalize as a *co-treatment* arrangement: both names on cases, both names on patient consent forms, shared decision-making, formal fee split.
- **Why Gaby:** Builds a *partnership* not just a referral relationship. Patients get two clinicians; both names get attributed.
- **Risk:** Only with colleagues whose standards you trust 100%.
- **Effort:** 1 week to draft per co-treatment contract; template at `dentist/05_OPERATIONS/legal-compliance/practice-legal/`.

# §B — Remote-second-opinion program (formalized)

> The Ohio hospital case (53% conversion within 15 months) and Picasso Dental (65% conversion from remote consults) make this the single highest-leverage *new* idea in this v2.

### B.1 — Formal Remote Second-Opinion (RSO) program
- **Source case:** One Ohio hospital reported that **53% of second-opinion patients become hospital patients within 15 months**. Picasso Dental Vietnam reports 65% conversion from free remote consults.
- **What:** Build a formal program, not ad-hoc:
  - **Free 15-min triage** via WhatsApp to qualify
  - **Paid Written Second Opinion** at Gs 800k–1.2M (5-7 days turnaround)
  - **Conversion to in-person consult** at 53% rate (Ohio data) = 5-10 patients/year per 10 RSO engagements
- **Why Gaby:** Her existing differentiation is ALREADY second-opinion escrita. Formalizing this into a 1-page productized offering is 1 page of work + a Stripe/Pagopar invoice link.
- **Why this works:** Patients who *invest* in the paid RSO are self-qualified as high-conversion cases. They don't even need to be sold to — they've already raised their hand.
- **Source:** Mentioned `gaby-client-engine/01-playbook/LESSONS-FROM-DENTISTS-WORLDWIDE.md` #1 informally; this v2 formalizes the math.
- **Effort:** 1 day to assemble (intake form + delivery SOP + invoice). Continuous operation.

### B.2 — "Written Plan Only" remote tier (no in-person required)
- **Source case:** Picasso's lower-tier offers "review of records + X-rays you already have" at lower price than full fresh CBCT scan.
- **What:** Three-tier pricing:
  - **Tier 1** (free, 15 min WhatsApp triage): if patient just wants orientation
  - **Tier 2** (Gs 600k, "Written Plan Only"): patient mails/emails existing X-ray + treatment plan from current dentist; Gaby provides written second opinion in 5-7 days without in-person consult
  - **Tier 3** (Gs 1.5M, "Full In-Person Second Opinion"): patient comes in + new CBCT + written plan + 30-min walkthrough
- **Why Gaby:** Tier 2 is the new moat — cheap for patient to try, fully written + signed, easy to scale remotely without chair time.
- **Effort:** 4h setup, ongoing delivery.

### B.3 — "I'll see your X-ray free, no appointment" CTA — the lead magnet
- **Source case:** Picasso Vietnam starts 100% of their RSO funnel with "send us your OPG and we'll respond in 48 hours, free."
- **What:** A homepage hero CTA on ometzdental.com: "Send your panoramic X-ray + plan quote — get a free 15-minute written second opinion from a conservative dentist." Email upload via Google Form.
- **Why Gaby:** Not only captures leads; it positions Gaby as the alternative to the chain-clinic "just accept the treatment plan" pressure.
- **Effort:** 2h with existing assets (form, prompt, delivery SOP).

### B.4 — Second Opinion → Full Treatment Conversion Track
- **Source case:** Hospital RSO programs that report high conversion track the patient through:
  1. Inquiry (Day 0)
  2. Records received (Day 2)
  3. RSO delivered (Day 7)
  4. Phone walkthrough (Day 8)
  5. Decision conversation (Day 14)
  6. **First in-person visit (Day 21-30)**
  7. Treatment plan accepted (Day 30-45)
- **What:** Document this exact workflow as Gaby's SOP. Don't leave it loose.
- **Effort:** 2h to write down.

### B.5 — Hospital RSO Learning: Position through "thought leadership" content
- **Source case:** The Purview.net hospital RSO playbook recommends publishing "content relating to ordinary questions patients might ask about this specialty on a regular basis."
- **What:** Gaby publishes 1 FAQ-style blog post per month answering questions patients with second opinions actually ask:
  - "¿Cuánto vale un implante en Paraguay?"
  - "¿Qué preguntar antes de un tratamiento mayor?"
  - "5 señales de que necesitas una segunda opinión"
- **Why Gaby:** Every FAQ post is a permanent asset that pulls in second-opinion seekers 6-12-24 months later.
- **Effort:** 4-6h per post. Use dental AI scribes to draft; Gaby reviews.

# §C — Google Business Profile + Map Pack domination (2026 dental-specific)

> Two strong 2026 guides on this: RevenueWell + My Social Practice + Titan Web Agency. Common factors: GBP completeness + reviews velocity + photo updates + Q&A seeding.

### C.1 — Weekly GBP posts (currently: monthly cadence is the standard; weekly = competitive differentiator)
- **Source case:** My Social Practice confirms "weekly GBP posts are now the standard for active, visible listings" in 2026.
- **What:** Shift from monthly (default) to weekly GBP posts (52/year). Already have 26 posts ready in `dentist/06_MARKETING/gbp-posts/26-gbp-posts-6-meses.md`; build 26 more for year 2.
- **Why Gaby:** GBP ranking is won by active signals; weekly > monthly > never.
- **Effort:** 30 min/week for the 12 months.

### C.2 — GBP Photo Updates (quarterly, not just "set and forget")
- **Source case:** RevenueWell + Titan both emphasize photo upload velocity for ranking.
- **What:** 2-3 new photos uploaded to GBP every quarter:
  - 1 procedural case photo (with consent)
  - 1 staff/consultorio photo
  - 1 result/team-photo
- **Why Gaby:** Photo count + recency are explicit ranking signals (per Titan Web Agency).
- **Effort:** 30 min/quarter.

### C.3 — GBP Q&A Seeding (plant your own questions)
- **Source case:** Common GBP tactic — seed your own Q&A so patients see your answers before they ask.
- **What:** Login to GBP and seed:
  - Q: "¿Acepta seguros sociales?" A: [your answer]
  - Q: "¿Habla inglés?" A: [your answer]
  - Q: "¿Cuánto cuesta una limpieza?" A: [your answer with range]
  - Q: "¿Atiende urgencias?" A: [your answer with process]
  - Q: "¿Ofrece segunda opinión?" A: [your answer — major differentiator]
  - Q: "¿Trabaja los sábados?" A: [your answer]
- **Why Gaby:** You own these questions now — competitors won't be able to "own" them with their own planted answers.
- **Effort:** 30 min, one-time + maintenance.

### C.4 — GBP Q&A surveillance (weekly review)
- **What:** Weekly check: any new questions from patients? Any patients answering OTHER patients' questions incorrectly? Reply with official position.
- **Why Gaby:** GBP Q&A becomes a public knowledge graph over time. Patient answers can include misinformation ("¿Cuánto cuesta una corona? responde: 500 mil — la verdad: 800 mil"). Gaby needs to control this surface.
- **Effort:** 5 min/week.

### C.5 — GBP Service Description Optimization (per-service)
- **What:** Each of Gaby's ~15 services has its own GBP service entry with:
  - Description with target keywords
  - Price range (where reasonable)
  - Photo or video
  - Link to the website service page
- **Why Gaby:** Each service is its own ranking surface. A "blanqueamiento dental Asunción" search should hit Gaby's GBP service.
- **Effort:** 2h one-time.

### C.6 — NAP Consistency Audit (Name, Address, Phone)
- **Source case:** RevenueWell: "Inconsistent NAP information is one of the most common local SEO mistakes." Both Titan and HIP confirm.
- **What:** Audit all 8-10 directory listings (GBP, Waze, Apple Maps, Doctoralia, Top Doctors, Bing Places, LinkedIn, Yelp, Facebook). Ensure exact same name + address + phone as on website.
- **Why Gaby:** NAP inconsistency is a free ranking loss.
- **Effort:** 3h one-time.

### C.7 — UTM-tagged GBP link for outcome tracking
- **Source case:** My Social Practice: "Add UTM codes to the website links in your Google Business Profile."
- **What:** Add `?utm_source=GBP&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gaby` to all GBP action links.
- **Why Gaby:** Google Analytics then attributes every GBP-driven click.
- **Effort:** 5 min, lifetime value.

# §E — B2B broker referral contract structures

> The "WeWork Broker Partnership ToS" + Commercial Capital + Kitt examples are the gold standards for formal referral contracts. Gaby can copy these structures.

### E.1 — Formal "Specialist Referral Partner Agreement" template
- **Source case:** Multiple B2B broker programs (WeWork, Kitt, Commercial Capital) detail exact contract clauses.
- **What:** A formal 2-page agreement for each cross-referral partner that defines:
  - **§1 Submission process** (how leads are referred)
  - **§2 Acceptance window** (10-day standard — references Commercial Capital's "10-day acceptance window" best practice)
  - **§3 Approved lead definition** (covers same entity, affiliates, subsidiaries)
  - **§4 Fee trigger** (when payment obligation activates — first paid appointment)
  - **§5 Net commission calculation** (5-10% of first-month invoice, paid within 30 days)
  - **§6 Non-circumvention** (you can't bypass the partner with that specific patient for 12 months)
  - **§7 Tail period** (12-month tail = partner gets credit for any lead submitted before termination)
  - **§8 Confidentiality** (no disclosure of fee structure)
  - **§9 Termination** (30-day notice)
- **Why Gaby:** Most dentists do referral exchanges informally; this formalizes. Actually **creates economic incentive** for partners to actively refer — even small 5% commissions compound across decades.
- **Effort:** 1 day to draft one master template, replicate per partner.

### E.2 — "Active Representation" obligation (the rare clause)
- **Source case:** WeWork ToS requires "Active Representation" — the partner has to actively engage with the lead, not just forward a name.
- **What:** For high-value referrals (implants, full rehab), require the partner dentist to actively engage with the patient before Gaby's office takes over.
- **Why Gaby:** Filters out low-quality referrals. Vetted partners are worth the higher commission.
- **Effort:** Boilerplate addition to the contract template.

### E.3 — Tiered commission based on case value
- **Source case:** Commercial Capital offers "10%-50% commission splits" depending on deal structure.
- **What:** Gaby's commission to referring partner:
  - Hygiene / recall visits: 0% (base service, no incentive needed)
  - Restorative cases (coronas, carillas): 5% of case value
  - Implants, rehab, full-mouth: 8-10% of case value (high-value case)
  - Coaching / consultation referral: USD 50 flat
- **Why Gaby:** Higher commission on higher-value cases is appropriate; incentivizes partners to refer BIG cases, not tiny ones.
- **Effort:** Add to contract template.

### E.4 — Annual contract "Bazaar"-style networking lunch
- **Source case:** Kitt's referral program structure implies ongoing partner relationships; many broker programs run an annual in-person summit.
- **What:** Once Gaby has 30 active referral partners, host an annual lunch (Gs 1-3M cost) where all 30 attend for 3 hours of discussion:
  - Year-in-review
  - Best referrals of the year
  - Plans for next year
  - Hand out "Top Partner" recognition (1-2 awards)
- **Why Gaby:** Loyalty + retention × recognition = next year's best referrals coming from the same partners.
- **Effort:** 1 week to plan + 1 day to execute.

### E.5 — Patient-broker relationship: explicit "thank-you" commission
- **Source case:** Referral fee agreements standard practice in B2B; not standard in patient referrals but emerging.
- **What:** Existing patient who refers a friend gets:
  - 0-3 friends/year: Gs 50k discount on next cleaning
  - 4-10 friends/year: Gs 100k credit toward any service
  - 11+: Gs 200k annual dental package credit
- **Why Gaby:** Etymology: existing patients become the broker network.
- **Effort:** 30 min setup; depends on tracking.

# §H — Online review-response architecture (HIP-style templates)

> Both RevenueWell and HIP dental agency prioritize review responses as the #1 local SEO lever.

### H.1 — Review-response script library (HIP-style templates)
- **Source case:** HIP dental agency provides a "Front Desk Script" for review requests. Healthcare providers should respond to every review with HIPAA-safe but warm language.
- **What:** Draft 5 review-response templates for each scenario:
  1. 5-star review: thank + mention service + city naturally
  2. 4-star review: thank + ask what would have made it 5 star
  3. 3-star review: apology + offer to call + specific offer
  4. 2-star review: professional acknowledgement + public note of follow-up
  5. 1-star review: refusal-to-debate + private channel mention (NEVER argue publicly)
- **Why Gaby:** Google weights review responses. A 1-star with a great company response ranks higher than a 1-star with no response.
- **Effort:** 4h to draft.

### H.2 — Review request timing (HIP recommendation: front-desk at appointment close)
- **Source case:** HIP: "Review request email sent automatically to every client at case close — with direct link to their GBP review form."
- **What:** Kiki WhatsApps every patient 24h after their appointment with: "Gracias por visitarnos. Si tuviste una buena experiencia, me ayudaría mucho una reseña en Google: [link]. 30 segundos."
- **Why Gaby:** Review velocity (not just count) is the second-biggest ranking factor.
